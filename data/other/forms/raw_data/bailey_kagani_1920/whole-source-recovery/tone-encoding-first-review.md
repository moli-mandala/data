# Source tone notation: first original-page review

Bailey printed87–89 explains that his ordinary h-based spelling and the
demonstration spellings on88 are two representations of the same words. A
perpendicular stroke above the vowel represents high tone, and one below the
vowel represents deep tone. The author discusses pitch movement in prose;
these transcriptions do not replace those descriptions with inferred IPA tones.

The first-reading ledger preserves both representations. Proposed literal
Unicode encoding is U+030D COMBINING VERTICAL LINE ABOVE and U+0329 COMBINING
VERTICAL LINE BELOW. They describe the printed shapes here, **not** the IPA
meanings those combining marks may have elsewhere. A separate raised apostrophe
is retained as U+2019 where the original prints it. Existing readable macrons,
breves and nasal marks remain; a macron is not supplied where the demonstration
replaces it with a perpendicular stroke.

Root compared all twelve pairs and two optional-tone pairs in the original
page, including6x crops. Reproduction: `tmp/kagani-whole/render_details.py`,
printed88 rects(22,329,308,423), (22,421,308,484), (25,384,307,405),
(21,403,307,431). Original PDF hash is pinned in scope-manifest.json.

Both literal spellings remain first-reading proposals. Independent review and
the source-specific profile must check these sequences before installation.
The existing glossary profile must not discard a mark as a syllabicity sign
or convert a tone mark to a phonological interpretation. Source88 explicitly
places the sister-in-law deep-tone stroke under long ā and brother-in-law
under ī; a blanket first-vowel rule would be wrong.
