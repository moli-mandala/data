Permissive mood is expressed by the suffix-kitu  
varikitu 
‘let come’ 
optative marker is -katte. 
mantukatte 
‘let him run’ 
 
Purposive forms take –parru. 
kaamparru ‘for seeing’ 
 
Potential forms have –kaam (cf. Ma.- aam) 
kikaam ‘let us go’ 
 
Verbal participles have two suffixes, -u and  -ee 
naakku ‘ having laughed’ 
varaattee ‘not having come’ 
 
Conditional is marked by aante 
untaante ‘ while drinking’ 
 
Veda speech also has a copula verb in  aata which is unattested in other languages, but could 
be related to the Malayalam forms –aak, aan, etc. 
avaru caliccantu kattaata 
‘what he said is a lie’ 
avarite peeru kooran enraata 
‘his name is Koran’ 
 
Interrogative forms take –ee suffix. 
atuvu varutavee? ‘ is she coming’ 
omme calikkaamee? 
‘can we talk?’ 
 
The interrogative pronoun evite of Malayalam has as its corresponding from in Veda eente. 
 
avaru eente vattave? ‘where does they live?’ 
iyyu eente kayteeni? 
‘where are you going?’ 
 
Affirmative questions use the form eelaa (cf. Ma. allee?illee?) 
 
avaru keesavaraataa  eelaa? ‘ he is Kesavan, is he not? 
avaru kaliyanrave eelaa?  ‘they will go, will they not?’ 
-alle and ille are negative forms.  
atuvu enra makalalle ‘she is not my daughter’ 
eneeru penaaya ille 
‘I don’t have a pen’
