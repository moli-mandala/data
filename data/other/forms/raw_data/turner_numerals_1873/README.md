# Turner 1873 numeral note: analysis only

H. G. T. [H. G. Turner], “Remark on the Note concerning Ancient Dravidian Numerals,” *Indian Antiquary* 2 (March 1873), printed p. 97, is available in the [public-domain original volume](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:The_Indian_Antiquary_Vol_2.pdf) (PDF physical page 76 in that scan). The article was screened in full against the original page and its [OCR transcription](https://jainqq.org/booktext/Indian_Antiquary_Vol_02/032494).

The seven printed numeral series contain 67 prompt cells: Koi 10, Gadaba 9, Kerang Kapu 20, Pengu Porja 5, Durwa Gonds 9, Selliya Porja 6, and Tagara Porja 8. Two additional Gadaba numerals (8 and 9) occur in the prose, giving **69 source tokens**. There are no other lexical examples in Turner's note. Koi, Pengu Porja, Durwa Gonds, Selliya Porja, and Tagara Porja are explicitly grouped by Turner as Dravidian controls; they account for 38 cells. Gadaba and Kerang Kapu are grouped by Turner as Kolarian (31 tokens, including the two prose Gadaba forms).

**No cells are installed.** Generic historical “Gadaba” cannot yet be mapped safely to a canonical modern Gadaba lect, despite the Munda-like numerals and Turner's Kolarian classification. The Kerang Kapu identification is materially contested: Francis associated the numeral system with Bonda Poraja, while Glottolog's bibliography of Thurston and Rangachari (1909) records an editor's judgment that the forms could be Gtaʔ. Thurston also quotes Turner's earlier Kerang Kapu description but does not establish a unique modern language. Language mapping requires independent attribution, not a match of a few forms. The article is held as a complete-source screening, with all 69 tokens accounted for by series, while the existing partial Munda ingestions are completed first.
