034: Second person pronouns in Darai are characterized by three levels of 
035: honorificity. When an honorific pronoun is used for the referent, the verb is likewise 
036: marked.20  The second person pronoun with the root tə is common in middle Indo-
037: Aryan (MIA) on account of contamination (Chatterji cited in Jha 1958: 394). The 
038: etymology of the high grade honorific pau ‘you’ is difficult to trace.21  
039: Honorificity (politeness) in Darai is basically dependent on whether the speech 
040: act participants are kins or kiths. Generally one is not obliged to use honorifics within 
041: the non-affinal relationship irrespective of age and seniority. The pronoun toi  can be 
042: used to address any family members (kinsmen). Thus it can be used to address 
043: parents, brothers and sisters. This is the pronoun used in communicative settings 
044: between equals and addressing children by elders or parents.  
045: However, daughter-in-laws and sister-in-laws are addressed using the pronoun 
046: tohe. The high honorific pronoun pau can be used to address strangers, to address 
047: someone very formally and when the speech act participants are in-laws.
