003: vi. Case markers and postpositions 
004:   
005: Darai nouns are marked for cases in two ways: inflection and postpositions. 
006: There are a few case markers and other case relation is carried out by postpositions. 
007: Masica (1991:230) maintains, “In NIA, case is vitally a syntactic as well as a 
008: morphological category” and further adds that it is “a problematic nominal category in 
009: NIA, whether for cross-linguistic, historical, or single-language descriptive analysis.”   
010:  
011: We will base the discussion of our study on two main argumentations. 
012: Lilgegren (2008:161) notes: 
013: Firstly, a string of segments with its own pitch accent would be considered a 
014: separate word. This would place the longer words with two or more syllables 
015: into distinct postpositions, not case inflections. On the other hand, 
016: monosyllabic markers which are attached to the head noun they occur with are 
017: case inflection rather than words. Secondly, inflectional suffixes obligatorily 
018: follow the nouns.  
019: This leads to the argumentation that some postpositions follow the genitive 
020: marker (see Masica 1991: 231-32; Lilgegren 2008: 161).11 
021: On the basis of this theoretical orientation, there are case markers for dative-
022: accusative, locative, genitive, ablative, comitative and ergative-instrumental. They are 
023: monosyllabic and are directly attached to the noun. Nouns are either base (null 
024: marked) or followed by a case marker.  
025: A first look into Darai shows that it is a nominative-accusative language.
