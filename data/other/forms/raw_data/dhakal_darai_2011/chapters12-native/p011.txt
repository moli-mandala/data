010: Gautam and Magar (1994:157) mention that ‘Darai’ is derived from daras 
011: ‘administrative regions’. Prior to the unification of Nepal by Late King Prithvi 
012: Narayan Shah, various regions in the Karnali region were divided into various daras. 
013: They speculate that it is from daras that the word Darai seems to have originated.  
014: However, Adhikari (2041VS:1) explains that the kingdom of Darais has not been  
015: reported in any historical records of Gandaki region. Despite some scholars' claim that 
016: Darais had a kingdom in Gorkha, there are no corroborative accounts to lend support 
017: to it. 
018: Gautam and Magar (1994:158) further note that the name of Darais is also 
019: found in the Mahabharat, Manusmriti and Haribanshapurana. Contrary to this claim, 
020: Adhikari opines that the people named ‘Darad’ mentioned in these holy scripts are 
021: Aryan in origin and are still living in Dardistan (Adhikari 2041VS). He further adds 
022: that the Darais living in Nepal are Mongoloid in origin. Since some of the people 
023: settled in terai, they were named ‘Darai’ (2041VS:2). Similar view is articulated by 
024: Pokhrel (1995:5). He notes that these people might have walked up the inner terai and 
025: hills along the banks of the Narayani river. These migrated terai dwellers were called 
026: Darai. Thus, the term 'Darai' is also a loconym. These conjunctures, however, do not 
027: convincingly resolve questions regarding the origin of the Darai. This is a topic that 
028: continues to be a disputed subject.

030: 2.1.2 People and profession 
031: Gautam and Magar (1994:157) note, "Darais are of Mongloid stock with short 
032: stature, depressed nasal ruts and of which they have stumpy nose, oblique eyes and 
033: dark skinned." Bista (1967:117) adds, "In their appearance, attitudes and behavior 
034: they are not much different from Tharus. They are very dark and seemingly bony: yet 
035: they display great health and strength."  He further adds that the Darais are culturally 
036: and linguistically a homogeneous population.
