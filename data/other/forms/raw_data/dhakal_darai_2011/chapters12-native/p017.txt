003: 2.1.4 Darai house 
004:  
005: The uniqueness of Darai is found in their language, home, puja 'worship' and 
006: specific rites and rituals (Darai 1996: 40). The construction of a traditional house 
007: follows a consistent pattern and it is strictly prescribed.  
008:  
009:  
010:  
011: PHOTOGRAPH 2.1 Traditional Darai houses 
012:  
013: Traditional Darai houses are rectangular in shape. They are one-storey 
014: buildings made up of wooden pillars and beams. The more beams a house has the 
015: broader and longer it becomes. The partition from the central wooden beam is 
016: important because there is the place of worshipping in the corner near the central 
017: beam. Instead of wall of cement or stone, houses are made up of twigs. The partitions 
018: are plastered by the paste of mud and cow dung. There are no big windows except 
019: small holes to let the light pass through them as can be seen in Photograph (2.1). The 
020: length of the house is not fixed but may fluctuate according to the need of the family.  
021: The house has əsəra ‘porch’.  əsəra ‘porch’ accommodates the grinding stone, 
022: seating of guests at their arrival as well as seating of the untouchable castes. On the 
023: side of the əsəra there is a place pənəici  which accommodates the water pot. It is 
024: attached to the front side of the house. əsəra may also be attached to ə̃ɡna ‘yard’ in 
025: the front of the house.   
026:  
027: Two doors are placed on the right side of the house at the front and back, 
028: across the breadth of the house to allow the air pass across ka⟦U+F0E2⟧dbari, or across the 
029: doors. There are two kinds of doors used in Darai houses.  While the doors made up 
030: of wood are called ke⟦U+F0E2⟧wari, the doors made up of twigs and muds are called pʰokra.
