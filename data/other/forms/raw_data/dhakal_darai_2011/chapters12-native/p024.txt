022: 2.2.3 Politeness strategies 
023: Politeness strategies are important in the hierarchical societies in Nepal to 
024: learn the rules of verbal politeness. Abbi (2001:232) comments, “We grant that it is an 
025: highly relevant phenomenon and politeness has to be somehow communicated, as 
026: sentences/discourse lacking such communication may be taken as impolite.”  
027: Abbi (2001:332-335) points out some strategies in maintaining politeness in 
028: Indian societies. The speakers choose certain lexical elements (such as honorifics, 
029: personal pronouns and kin terms). Syntactic features in nominal and verb morphology 
030: have specific constructions. The use of appropriate pronouns for the addressees, or the 
031: referents while using the second person and third person pronouns is strictly 
032: prescribed (see Chapter 4) at lexical level. The use of honorific pronouns in Darai 
033: differs from Nepali. The honorific pronouns are mainly used to address the in-laws. 
034: Similarly, the use of appropriate pronominal possessive suffixes in genitive 
035: construction (see Chapter 4) and appropriate inflections in verb morphology (see 
036: Chapter 6) indicate whether the addressee and referent are shown by respect or not. 
037: The addresser should not use certain discourse particles when he addresses the people 
038: he shows respects (see 8.2.7).
