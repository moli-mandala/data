013:  
014: Rest of the house is generally divided into partitions or ‘rooms’ to 
015: accommodate all family members. The kitchen is strictly placed on the right corner of 
016: the house while entering the house from the front door. The fireplace is a shallow hole 
017: in the ground with three stones stand over the top. Just above the cooking area hangs a 
018: potka 'shelf' which is used to smoke pieces of meat and to keep things out of the way 
019: of insects.  
020: One more important place for the Darais is the central wooden beam and 
021: pillar. They divide the house exactly into two near the kitchen. This is the place of 
022: ancestral shrine called ɡənbaba 'the ancestors'. The ritual worship to the ancestors is 
023: performed at this spot. This is the place where the worship to the house god is 
024: performed but otherwise has no other function.

026: 2.1.5 Naming ceremony 
027:  
028: A woman who gives birth to a baby is attended by ɡʰər ɡurəu ‘shaman’ and 
029: suḍʰerni ‘midwife’.  They have traditional belief that complication in giving  
030: birth to a child may be made easy by them. The attendant helps the mother during a 
031: few weeks after the birth. The rituals related to uṭʰəuni 'naming ceremony' is 
032: performed on the eleventh day if a boy is born whereas on the ninth day if it is a 
033: daughter.  A Brahnim priest (the priest belonging to the upper caste in social caste 
034: system) is not necessary for Darais for ritual performances and naming a child. 
035: Instead, either the maternal uncle of the newly born baby or mother performs the 
036: conventional rituals. The newly born child and mother take baths before other rituals
