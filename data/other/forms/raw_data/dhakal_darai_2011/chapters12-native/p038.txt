003: 2.3.10 Governmental and institutional language policies12 
004: UNESCO (2003:12) mentions: 
005: The programs related to language education and literacy are not very common 
006: in new work environments, new media, including broadcast media and the 
007: internet. They usually serve only to expand the scope and power of the 
008: dominant language at the expense of endangered languages.  
009: Darai people have very positive attitudes towards all these domains. Although 
010: there is constitutional stipulation for the preservation of minority languages, they are 
011: not protected, or properly planned to be protected to preserve them. The following are 
012: the provisions stipulated in the Interim Constitution of Nepal 2064 VS (2007): 
013: a. All the mother-tongues spoken in Nepal are the national languages of the 
014: country (Article 5.1). 
015: b. The Nepali language in Devanagari script shall be the official language 
016: (Article 5.2). 
017: c. Right to receive basic education in the mother-tongue as provided for in 
018: the law (Article 17.1). 
019: d. Right to preserve and promote its own language and cultural heritage 
020: (Article 17.3). 
021: The language falls in 'passive assimilation' into this UNESCO category.

023: 2.3.11 Community members' attitudes towards their language13 
024: Majority of the speakers feel that their language is sweet. Contrary to these 
025: statements, majority of the respondents (80 out of 105 or 80.95 percentage) do not 
026: feel that their language is rich. Majority of respondents (85 out of 105 or 76.19 
027: percentage) are optimistic about the continuation of the language when the children 
028: who are now young grow up and become old. In this study, most of them are from 
029: Chitwan and Tanahun. All the speakers feel that they feel bad when they hear Darai 
030: young people speaking other languages instead of Darai.  When this situation is 
031: applied to the UNESCO factors, many members support language maintenance;
