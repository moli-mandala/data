009: The study on Darai language is found in  Darai Phonemic Summary (Kotapish 
010: and Kotapish 1975). They present minimal pairs, vowels and consonants, consonant 
011: clusters and positional variants (intervocalic position of consonants, word medial 
012: (preconsonantal) and word final position). This is the first serious investigation of the 
013: Darai phonology. This study brings a wealth of facts about Darai phonology. This 
014: study serves as an outstanding starting point for anyone interested in Darai 
015: phonology. However, some phonological features evident in the Chitwan dialect are 
016: not mentioned in this study. For instance, we find consonant sounds such as /lʰ/, /mʰ/, 
017: /nʰ/ and /rʰ/ in intervocalic position in the Chitwan dialect which they (1975) have 
018: not mentioned. 
019: Kotapish (1973) analyses the cases in Darai. She reports that the nominative 
020: case is unmarked, ergative case is coded with -i, accusative is coded with 
021: -ke, locative with -jə and ablative with the postposition maṭe 'from'. Thus, she 
022: analyses the major aspects of the case relation and case markers. When it is compared 
023: with the Chitwan dialect, it exhibits some contrasts. Firstly, she mentions that the first 
024: person singular also codes ergativity (1973:28) which is absent in the Chitwan dialect. 
025: Secondly, she notes that all nouns except kinship terms have no inflection in the 
026: accusative. We have some counter examples for this statement from our discourse 
027: data, such as ɡoru-ke 'ox-ACC', bʰotʰi-ke 'Bhothi fish-ACC'. Thus, the 'accusative 
028: case' can be extended to animate nouns. Thirdly, the ablative is coded with the 
029: ablative marker -se  'from' and the postposition maṭe 'from' in the Chitwan dialect. 
030: She simply mentions the postposition maṭe 'from' as the ablative marker.
