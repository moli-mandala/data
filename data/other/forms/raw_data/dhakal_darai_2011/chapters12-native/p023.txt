011: 2.2.2 Forms of address 
012: Terms of address in Darai society are determined by the parameters such as 
013: age and kinship terms. Two kinds of forms of address are employed in Darai: direct 
014: and indirect. Generally, the direct forms are employed and acceptable only for juniors 
015: or contemporaries. Ferguson (1992:32) notes: 
016: Generally a wife does not address or refer to her husband either by kin term or 
017: by name. She may use a special particle of address, or make use of an 
018: expression such as 'Father of X', X being their first child or some other 
019: circumlocution.  
020: Addressing a husband by his name is not acceptable. A husband does not 
021: address his wife by name. They address their spouses by calling the child’s name plus 
022: mother/father (e.g. X’s mother/father). The parents can address their children by 
023: names or nicknames. Abbi (2001:226) remarks, “Although every society has its 
024: norms for selecting appropriate forms of address and referral, the hierarchical 
025: structure of the Indian society offers a wide and complex range of options to choose 
026: from.”   
027:  
028: i. Direct address 
029: There are mainly three ways of direct address. To begin with, direct address is 
030: used in discourse. The first name is generally employed to the addressees (speech act 
031: participants) junior to the addresser. Contrary to this, if the addressee is senior to the 
032: addresser, the kinship terms are employed for the direct address. If the rule is violated, 
033: the situation is called hawa mante ‘addressing without maintaining proper manner’. 
034: Proper names are thus often employed for addressing juniors and intimate addressees. 
035: However, addressing the seniors using the proper names is not considered appropriate 
036: in some occasions. And, the relatives are formally addressed with kinship terms.
