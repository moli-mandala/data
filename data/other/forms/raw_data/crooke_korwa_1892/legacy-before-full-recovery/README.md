# Crooke 1892 Mirzapur Korwa glossary

William Crooke's independent four-page glossary was elicited from an unnamed bilingual Korwa speaker in the southern part of Mirzapur. The [Biodiversity Heritage Library article record](https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/part/368034) explicitly marks this article public domain. Its catalog dates the item 1893, while the printed running heads and volume contents say 1892. The [journal scan](https://rapeutation.com/journasiasoc1892a.pdf) was checked visually at PDF pp. 135–138 = printed pp. 125–128, SHA-256 in `manifest.json`.

`reviewed_inventory.tsv` transcribes all 123 Crooke form–gloss lines. It selects 93 direct lexical attestations and holds 30 multiword, inflected, uncertain or disjunctive items. The last 13 Crooke lines are at the top of p. 128. The separate W. H. P. Driver article and Korwa/Korku comparison below the rule are excluded. The raw romanization retains Crooke's circumflexes and other visual distinctions; the importer does not invent IPA. Crooke's explicit Hindi, Sanskrit, English and Kuri comparisons remain notes and are not graph edges.

A page-stratified manual recheck of 20 printed lines (six each from pp. 125–127 and two from p. 128) is recorded in `visual-sample-20260925.jsonl`: 19 selected and one held row, with zero material accepted-form errors. The profile projects circumflex vowels to house long vowels and keeps the printed spelling in the source CSV; it does not infer a more detailed IPA analysis.

Run `UV_CACHE_DIR=/private/tmp/jambu-uv-cache uv run python data/other/forms/raw_data/crooke_korwa_1892/import_source.py` from `data/` to regenerate the source-local CSV and JSONL audit. Add `--install` only to copy the reviewed CSV into the forms input directory; this does not build a database. The precise speaker locality is unspecified, so the source-qualified Mirzapur dialect has no point coordinates. The 93 rows include the independent `lutur` 'ear' witness even though later Pinnow/Bahl sources attest the same form and gloss.
