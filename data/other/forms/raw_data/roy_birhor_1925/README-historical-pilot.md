Sarat Chandra Roy's original 1925 Ranchi edition, *The Birhors: A Little-Known Jungle Tribe of Chota Nagpur*, contains an Appendix I Birhor vocabulary on printed pp. 567–591. The [Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/birhors033490mbp) is 710 PDF pages, SHA-256 `424413333a05be576839d9a026477a7767080356d8534b19e951f411cb25837d`. Its original title page visibly says 1925. This package covers **only printed pp. 567–568 / PDF pp. 655–656**, whose public-domain page images are `printed-p567.png` and `printed-p568.png`. The whole PDF remains a local research cache.

Printed p. 567 has 23 two-column heads (L01–L09, R01–R14), and p. 568 has 36 (L01–L20, R01–R16). `reviewed-inventory.jsonl` records **all 59** in printed order, including raw Roman head, English gloss, comparison mark, page/column/item, visual check, and typed disposition. **Forty-six** are installed. **Thirteen** are held: six on p. 567 (one unexplained apostrophe-like segment and five `ch`/`chh` readings) and seven on p. 568 (one clustered consonant, five unclear underdot diacritics, and one apostrophe-like segment). The 20-entry seeded visual sample has no material print-reading errors among its selected rows. Source macrons are retained in `Original` and mapped to house macrons by the source-specific profile; this is transliteration of Roy's notation, not a new phonetic measurement.

Roy's footnote says **M.** marks Mundari with the same word and sense, **H.** Hindi, and **B.** Bengali. These remain source comparison notes in `Description`; no inferred loan, cognate, etymology, or graph edge is created. The two `ālāng` heads (*we two* and *tongue*) have distinct entry keys and senses. Roy's `āji` *grand-mother* is retained separately from Pinnow's `aji` *elder sister*.

`overlap-review.jsonl` compares all 59 Roy heads with the 323 Birhor forms in the preexisting compiled inventory, including 288 Pinnow 1959 and 35 Zoller 2023 entries. Nineteen Roy heads have a same/near form in the compiled inventory (three of these are held). Some Pinnow forms and glosses resemble Roy closely, but the available Pinnow-derived records have no underlying Birhor witness attribution. **Dependence on Roy is not established.** Accordingly, the sixteen selected parallel Roy heads keep their own citation; same spelling alone is not an exclusion reason. The comparison is not a claim of independent field elicitation by Pinnow.

The original 1925 edition is public domain in the United States (published before 1931) and India (Roy died 1942, so the life-plus-60 term expired). The checked-in page image permits direct review of the selected readings. `import_source.py --install` deterministically writes the CSV from the curated inventory and does **not** build the database. The source has no exact elicitation site or named sublect, so canonical Birhor is used without an invented dialect. Full source extraction and review are now active as described above.
