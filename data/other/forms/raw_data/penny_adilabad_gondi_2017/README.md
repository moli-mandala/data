# Western Southern / Adilabad Gondi numerals, 2013/2017

The primary source is Mark Penny's [Western Southern Gondi numeral page](https://lingweb.eva.mpg.de/channumerals/Gondi-Southern-Adilabad.htm) in Eugene Chan's numeral collection. Its heading names *Western Southern Gondi*, the Chinese heading clarifies *Adilabad Gondi*, and its own comments say “Western Southern Gondi or Adilabad Gondi.” It credits Mark Penny, SIL International, on 20 February 2013 and 9 February 2017. This import uses exact `raw/Gondi-Southern-Adilabad.htm` from [numeralbank/channumerals v1.0.1](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15649312), whose `raw/README.md` dates download to 12 February 2019 and whose `LICENSE` is CC BY 4.0. [Glottolog](https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/id/utno1237) treats Adilabad Gondi (`utno1237`, ISO `wsg`) as a Spoken L1 language distinct from Aheri Gondi (`aher1237`) and generic Jambu `Gondi` (`gond1265`). This exact Penny source is registered under the new `Adilabad Gondi` canonical. The source says India but gives no precise elicitation site, so canonical coordinates remain blank. Jambu already has a historical `adil` dialect under generic Gondi, quality-C Adilabad locality, with **758 compiled forms** from older DEDR material including Rao 1954 `SR-Gondi` and Burrow/Bhattacharya 1951 `A-Gondi`; 20260819 DEN files also use legacy pseudo-language `adil`. Those rows and their identifiers are **not migrated**. The parallel registry identity is intentional pending a full-build, source-by-source identity audit; it does not claim that the old forms belong to Penny's precise canonical.

The single 20-row, two-column table has **40 numbered cells**, all filled. Six cells print two independent answers (9, 10, 11, 16, 20, 60), and two cells print three (1000, 2000), giving **50 installed readings**. Slash, comma, and semicolon each divide explicit printed alternatives; no `Variant_Of_Key` direction is inferred. Eight individual answers are marked `< Indic` and receive `loanword`, but the source identifies no particular donor language/form, so no borrowed-from edge is made. The audit preserves each raw answer and whether it is individually annotated `< Indic`, `(native)`, or neither. In particular, the unmarked 2000 alternatives, `sare avʈ` at 28, and `pada avʈ` at 18 are **not** automatically tagged as loans merely because they contain an element elsewhere marked borrowed. The profile maps the page's IPA-like symbols to Jambu conventions (`d͡ʒ` and `dʒ` → `j`, `ɳ` → `ṇ`, `ɡ` → `g`, colon/IPA vowel length → macrons). `Phonemic` and raw `Original` preserve the source reading; `Form` changes only at the profile layer. The page's transcription-key prose, higher-number examples, and Chinese text are outside the numbered-cell scope. No OCR was used. All 50 forms remain unlinked because the source provides no etymon IDs or cognacy claims.
