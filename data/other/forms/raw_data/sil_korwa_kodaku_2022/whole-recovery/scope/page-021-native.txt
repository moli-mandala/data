2.5 
Conclusions 
Based on the above study, the following conclusions are made regarding the Pahadi Korwa, the 
Dihari Korwa, and the Kodaku people and language vitality. 
2.5.1 
The Pahadi Korwa 
The Korwa language is highly used in key domains among three-quarters of the Pahadi Korwa 
respondents, referred to as “Speaking” Pahadi Korwa,. There is another segment of the Pahadi Korwa 
population who are in a process of language shift toward the LWC Sadri, or are no longer using 
Pahadi Korwa at all 
The language attitude among those respondents who speak Pahadi Korwa is positive, while the 
other segment of the respondents no longer considers the Korwa language part of their identity. 
Nearly all respondents have a desire to see language development take place for their variety. 
2.5.2 
The Dihari Korwa 
Dihari Korwa respondents use Sadri in all domains. They have a positive attitude to Sadri, which 
they call their mother tongue. 
2.5.3 
The Kodaku 
The use of Kodaku in Chhattisgarh is greater than in Jharkhand. Language shift is in progress among 
the Kodaku respondents, especially in Jharkhand. 
The Kodaku respondents in Chhattisgarh have a comparatively stronger positive attitude toward 
the Kodaku language than do the Kodaku respondents in Jharkhand. However, both desire to see 
their language developed. The forming of Jharkhand as a separate state has revived the languages in 
the state. Hence, the Kodaku could potentially benefit from a language development programme.

Dialect areas 
One goal of the research was to discover the geographical distribution of Korwa and Kodaku speakers 
and determine the degree of linguistic similarity between the Korwa and Kodaku speech varieties. In 
Jharkhand, the Kodaku language is called Korwa. 
Dialect areas studies are often comprised of two components, namely  a lexical similarity study 
and dialect intelligibility testing. Using the lexical similarity counting procedures described by Blair 
(1990), any speech varieties showing lexical similarity percentages of less than 60 percent can be 
considered separate languages and thus there is no need for intelligibility testing. However, for 
speech varieties that have greater than 60 percent similarity, intelligibility testing should be done to 
determine their relationship (1990:23). 
The main focus of the present study was a lexical similarity study. In addition, a brief dialect 
intelligibility test was also done, especially in Jharkhand, to verify the findings from the lexical 
similarity study. 
3.1 
Lexical similarity 
Altogether, 25 wordlists were compared in this survey (see map 2): Given the overall focus of this 
research, nine Korwa wordlists and nine Kodaku lists were collected. In addition, a wordlist was 
collected in Tanmai village, Latehar, Jharkhand, was collected in a speech variety said to be Korwa. 
It seems, however, that this variety is a mixture of surrounding languages, such as Birjia, Mundari, 
and Sadri.  Respondents from Tanmai village confirmed these findings; they also reported that this 
variety has only very low number of speakers. 
To investigate the Sadri-Korwa lexical relationship, three Sadri wordlists were collected: 
standard Sadri from Kesramal (see also Abraham 1995), local Sadri from Dumortoli, and local Sadri, 
spoken by the Dihari Korwa from Kotgohna.
