Introduction 
Language is always closely related to any society and people group. It is said to be a hallmark of their 
identity. This sociolinguistic survey among the Korwa and Kodaku people of Chhattisgarh and 
Jharkhand was initiated in response to a possible need for vernacular development. The primary 
interest was to provide a clear idea about the sociolinguistic situation of Korwa and Kodaku for 
likeminded people who are interested in language development. The research was carried out by 
Gangadhar Behera, Jose P. Mathew, Binzy Joseph George, Liju Kuriakose, Sajayan V. Chacko, and 
Symon George, beginning in mid-June 2004 to September 2005 
As with its geographical diversity, India is extremely rich in languages, with languages falling 
into four language families. The Chhota Nagpur plateau is a very good example of both the 
geographical and the linguistic diversity in eastern India. It can be categorised as a multilingual 
geographical setting in India. The plateau covers much of Jharkhand state as well as adjacent parts of 
Orissa, Bihar, and Chhattisgarh. It is roughly situated between 22–24 degrees north latitude and 83–
85 degrees east longitude, which covers approximately 65,000 square kilometres. Much of this is 
covered with tropical forest, predominantly with the Sal tree. The plateau is one of the few 
remaining refuges left in India for tigers and Asian elephants (Wikipedia contributors 2022). 
This area is home for many people groups, who speak languages belonging to the Austro-Asiatic, 
Dravidian, and Indo-Aryan language families. Korwa and Kodaku are two tribes found in this region. 
According to Singh (1994:545), in the notification list of Scheduled Tribes for Madhya Pradesh, the 
Kodaku people have been grouped with the Korwa. However, Singh also mentions that, though the 
Kodaku and Korwa are often grouped together, they have distinct identities of their own and are two 
separate communities. This research agrees with the above statement. 
 
1.1 
Geography 
According to the Ethnologue (Gordon 2005:370), the Korwa people are found in the Chhattisgarh, 
Jharkhand, Orissa, Uttar Pradesh, West Bengal, Andhra Pradesh, and Maharashtra states of India. 
The main concentration is in Chhattisgarh and Jharkhand. In Chhattisgarh, they are found in 
Jashpur, Surguja, Raigarh, and Korba districts; in Jharkhand they are found in Gumla, Latehar, 
Palamau ,and Gadhwa districts (see map 1). 
The Kodaku people are found in Surguja district of Chhattisgarh, Palamau, and Gadhwa districts 
of Jharkhand, and Sonabhadra district of Uttar Pradesh). 
1.2 
History 
1.2.1 
The Korwa 
According to mythology, the Korwa tribe originated from the womb (Kora in their language) of a 
virgin goddess called Matha Koithama. It was in Ratanpur, when all the gods and goddess gathered 
together, that Matha Koithama, although being a virgin, gave birth to a baby.1 Since he was born in 
the presence of gods, this baby was known as Korwa (Powercity Korba n.d.c). 
According to another legend, the Korwa were created when god created man (the word kor 
means ‘man’). It is plausible that there is a relation between the word kor and the etymology of the 
word Korwa (Singh 1994:1826). Another source says that the Korwa tribe believe themselves to be a 
member of the Kauravs of Mahabharata where the Pando, a neighbouring tribe, claim to be members 
of the Pandav clan of Mahabharata (Surguja 2022).2 Other sources state that the name Korwa is 
derived from the term dodawas, meaning ‘earth diggers’ and their mother tongue is called by the 
same name (Singh 1994:613). Similarly, the name Kodaku is derived from Kodwa meaning ‘digger of 
the soil’ (1994:546). 
 
1 Ratanpur was the old capital of the Chhattisgarh state. It is situated approximately 25 km away from Bilaspur 
district headquarters. 
2 Kouravs (100 sons of Dhritarashter) and Pandovs (five brothers, sons of Pandu) were the royal cousins who 
fought for the kingdom in the epic of Mahabharata.
