3.3 
Conclusion 
The following points are extracted as the conclusion of dialect situation among the Korwa and 
Kodaku languages: The findings of the lexical similarity study suggest that the Pahadi Korwa and the 
Dihari Korwa use different languages as their mother tongue, namely Korwa and Sadri, respectively. 
The findings of the lexical similarity study also indicate that Korwa and Kodaku are different 
languages. There is an issue, however, regarding the use of autonyms: Korwa is the term used most 
widely in Jharkhand for both the Korwa and the Kodaku people and their languages. 
As for the Kodaku language, the findings suggest that the Baikanthpur variety might be 
considered the central variety, as it shares the highest lexical similarity with the other Kodaku 
varieties. Furthermore, the findings suggest that the Korwa variety spoken in the Rakkaya area might 
be considered the central dialect for the Korwa language, given that it shares the highest lexical 
similarity with the other Korwa varieties. 
 The findings from observations and informal interviews support these conclusions.

Bilingualism 
The ability to speak and understand two languages is called bilingualism. Blair (1990) says it is the 
knowledge and skills acquired by individuals, which enable them to use a second language other 
than their mother tongue. A second language may be acquired either formally (as in a school setting) 
or informally (through other types of contact). Bilingual proficiency, however, is not uniformly 
distributed within a community, as the level of proficiency varies among individuals and segments of 
the community (1990:52). It is closely related to demographic factors such as education, gender, age, 
and amount of travel, and depends upon their motivation and on the nature and extent of their 
contact with the second language. 
Therefore, it is always necessary to evaluate the bilingual proficiency of a community before 
attempting to make any decision regarding a project for vernacular language development. Hence, 
finding out the bilingual proficiency was one of the objectives in this survey. The following is a brief 
study of the bilingual proficiency of Korwa and Kodaku speakers.
