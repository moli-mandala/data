2.2.2 
Among the Dihari Korwa 
The question ‘What is your mother tongue, and did your forefathers have a different language than 
what you speak today?’ was asked to find out whether the Dihari Korwa respondents perceive any 
significant linguistic relation to Pahadi Korwa. All subjects reported Sadri to be their mother tongue, 
and they exclaimed that their forefathers never had a different language other than Sadri. In 
addition, almost all respondents expressed that their favourite language is their mother tongue 
(Sadri), and that it is good and pure. 
The responses to the question ‘If young people in your village do not speak Sadri, how would 
the old people feel?’ indicate that almost all the old subjects would be unhappy. For a few of them it 
does not matter, however. Young subjects all felt that the old people would feel unhappy if they do 
not speak Sadri, their mother tongue. Similarly, more than three-quarters of the total subjects 
reported that they will be unhappy if their children speak only Hindi, but all of them reported that 
they will be happy if their children read and write in their mother tongue, Sadri. In addition, when 
asked if the coming generations would continue to speak the language, all except one respondent 
said that they will surely speak their mother tongue. 
2.3 
Kodaku language use 
The summary of the language use pattern among Kodaku respondents differs from state to state. A 
questionnaire was administered to a total of 85 subjects, out of which 44 subjects were from 
Chhattisgarh and 41 from Jharkhand. People in Jharkhand call their language Korwa instead of 
Kodaku (see section 1.4). Therefore, the results presented in this section include the responses that 
called their mother tongue Korwa. 
Table 3 summarizes the reported language use patterns across different domains according to 
four language choice categories: Kodaku (including the responses as Korwa), K&S (Kodaku and 
Sadri), Sadri, and Other. Table 3 shows that the use of the Kodaku language is greater in 
Chhattisgarh compared with Jharkhand.

Most of the subjects from Chhattisgarh reported that they use their mother tongue in their 
homes. Three-quarters of them reportedly use Kodaku while speaking with the same caste in the 
village, and their children use it while playing. Kodaku and Sadri are used almost equally for private 
prayers as well as in marriage songs. Sadri is mainly used in such domains as the market, 
government office, and communicating with outsiders. 
The language use pattern in Jharkhand show a high use of Sadri. Use of the Kodaku language 
even in home domains is low. There is a notable drop in Kodaku use between the home domain and 
what children use while playing. These are possible indications of an increasing language shift to 
Sadri.
