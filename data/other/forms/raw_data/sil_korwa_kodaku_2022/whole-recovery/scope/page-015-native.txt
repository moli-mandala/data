Goal 1: Assess the vitality of both the Korwa and Kodaku languages 
Research questions: 
• How strongly is the mother tongue used by both the Pahadi Korwa and Kodaku people 
groups in important domains (home, village, market, neighbouring villages, government 
offices, religion, and school)? 
• What attitude do both Pahadi Korwa and Kodaku speakers have toward their mother tongue, 
toward each other’s languages, and toward Sadri? 
• Are they interested in mother tongue development? 
Tools: Questionnaires, RTT, post-RTT questions, interviews, and observation 
Goal 2: Appraise the dialect variation of both the Korwa and Kodaku languages 
Research questions: 
• What is the lexical similarity within the Korwa and Kodaku language varieties? 
• What is the lexical similarity between the Korwa and Kodaku language varieties? 
• How well do Korwa speakers understand a Kodaku story? 
• How well do speakers of Korwa living in Jharkhand understand a Korwa story recorded by 
Korwa speakers who live in Chhattisgarh? 
Tools: Wordlists, RTT, post-RTT questions, and observation 
Goal 3: Evaluate Sadri bilingualism levels among the Korwa and Kodaku people 
Research questions: 
• How well do Korwa and Kodaku people understand a story spoken in Sadri? 
• What is their reported proficiency in Sadri? 
• How well do Korwa and Kodaku people understand a story spoken in the variety of Sadri 
used locally? 
Tools: RTT, post-RTT questions, questionnaires, observation 
Goal 4: Map the geography where Korwa and Kodaku is spoken 
Research question: 
• What are the locations (taluks, districts, states, and country) where the Korwa and Kodaku 
speakers are found (geographical distribution)? 
Tools: Interviews, literature review, questionnaires, and observation

Language vitality 
Language vitality is a key element in any sociolinguistic research. It deals with the life of the 
language and its stability in succeeding generations. It relates to the overall strength of a language 
and its perceived usefulness in a wide variety of situations. Many variables contribute to vitality, 
such as patterns of language use and attitudes, native interest for language development, and the 
status of a language in the larger society. The purpose of assessing language vitality in Korwa and 
Kodaku is to throw light on the feasibility of language development (see Purpose, Goals, and 
Research questions in section 1.5). 
The study of language use patterns attempts to describe which languages or speech varieties 
members of a community use in different social domains. These domains are contexts in which the 
use of one language variety is considered more appropriate than another (Fasold 1984:183).
