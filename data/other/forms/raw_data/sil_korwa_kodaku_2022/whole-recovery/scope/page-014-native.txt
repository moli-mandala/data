1.3.4 
Religion 
Both the Korwa and Kodaku peoples are very religious. Even their origin links them to a famous 
Hindu religious text as they claim to be remnants of the Kauravs of the Mahabharata. The Korwa 
people worship various tribal gods and goddesses such as Khuria Rani, Dulha Deo, and Thakur Deo 
(Singh 1994:614). The Kodaku people worship local deities such as Gohel Dharti Mahadevi, Devi 
Bansapti, and Chalta (1994:546). 
According to Singh’s (1994) study of different census data (1961–1981), more than 98 percent 
of the Korwa people are categorised under Hinduism while the remaining 2 percent belong to other 
religions. Another source reports that almost 70 percent of Korwa are followers of Hinduism, while 
another 29 percent are Muslim and 1 percent are Christian (Bethany World Prayer Center 1997). The 
traditional religion of these people groups may be considered to be animism, as they worship nature 
and their ancestors, whom they believe to be their providers and protectors. In the case of Kodaku 
people, the researchers observed that most of them practice Hinduism, but there now seems to be a 
high percentage of Christians. 
The Karma festival is one of the biggest and most important religious celebrations of these 
tribes. It is usually conducted on alternate years and lasts from twenty-one days to a month. Apart 
from this they also celebrate Hindu festivals such as Holi, Diwali, Dussehra, and Ram Navami. 
1.4 
Language 
Korwa (ISO code [kfp]) and Kodaku (ISO code [ksz]) are classified in the Ethnologue (Gordon 2005) 
under the Austro-Asiatic language family. Korwa is the mother tongue of the Pahadi Korwa. Singh 
(1994:546) states that the mother tongue of Kodaku people is Korwa. According to the Ethnologue, 
ethnic Kodaku speak Korwa as their first language. The Ethnologue also states that Kodaku is a 
subgroup of Korwa. However, the present research reports that the Kodaku people speak Kodaku as 
their mother tongue. And in Jharkhand, the name Korwa is generally used even for Kodaku people 
and their language. That may be the reason why Singh (1994) and Gordon (2005) submit that the 
Kodaku people speak Korwa. 
As a second language, the Pahadi Korwa and Kodaku people speak Sadri (ISO code [sck]) 
(Gordon 2005), an Indo-Aryan language, that is widely spoken in the area. It primarily functions as 
the language of wider communication (LWC).  In some settlements, however, the Pahadi Korwa and 
Kodaku speak Sadri as their first language. The Dihari Korwa also speak Sadri as their first language. 
This justifies the information in the Ethnologue (Gordon 2005) that some ethnic Korwa speak Sadri or 
Chhattisgarhi as their first language.
