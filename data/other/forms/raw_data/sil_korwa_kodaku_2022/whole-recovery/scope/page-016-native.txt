The principal tool that was used for this study was a questionnaire on issues related to language 
use, attitude, and vitality. These questions were administered to mother tongue speakers in different 
villages. The subjects’ responses for each language are compiled. In addition, there were the informal 
interviews held and the observations made during fieldwork. (See Appendix A.)

Among the Pahadi Korwa, a total of 61 subjects were questioned, from which two categories are 
distinguished: those settlements where people speak Pahadi Korwa and those settlements where the 
speakers are shifting from Pahadi Korwa to Sadri.5 These categories were based on the overall 
responses regarding language use and attitude within each settlement. The surveyors found that 
there are Pahadi Korwa settlements where most of the people still speak Korwa. They are categorised 
as Speaking Korwa. There was also one settlement where most of the Pahadi Korwa people do not 
speak Korwa, even in key domains such as the home, traditional rituals, and in the village with other 
Korwa people. The Korwa people there speak Sadri. The 15 respondents from this village are 
categorised under Shifting Korwa. This segment of people has more contact with Sadri-speaking 
people and has a positive attitude toward using Sadri. Moreover, in this settlement only one or two 
very old people speak Korwa. The distribution of language use patterns in these villages seems to 
roughly represent the use of Korwa throughout the whole Pahadi Korwa population. 
Among the Dihari Korwa, only one village was selected. It was a typical Dihari Korwa village 
which the surveyors selected as representative of other Dihari Korwa settlements. Informal interviews 
and statements made by the Dihari Korwa people themselves supported this decision. A total of 19 
subjects took the test. 
As for the Kodaku language situation, the subject responses were also divided into two 
categories: Speaking Kodaku and Shifting Kodaku. Out of six villages covered in this study, three 
villages are located in Chhattisgarh state, while another three are situated in Jharkhand. The villages 
in Chhattisgarh still speak Kodaku and were categorised as Speaking Kodaku, while those in 
Jharkhand seem to be shifting to Sadri and were categorised as Shifting Kodaku. A total of 85 
subjects participated in the test and were almost equally distributed between the two states.
