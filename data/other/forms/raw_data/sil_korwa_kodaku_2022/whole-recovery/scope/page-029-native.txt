RTT results 
The RTT results for the Life story in local Sadri among the Pahadi Korwa testees show an average 
score of 88 percent, with a standard deviation of 12. This indicates that many testees were able to 
understand the story, while a few had difficulties. In other words, the Pahadi Korwa respondents at 
this test point display an adequate understanding of the local Sadri story. Considering the self-
reported questionnaires, those Pahadi Korwa who have more contact with outsiders generally are 
able to handle local Sadri. This category of people basically refers to the men, who generally learned 
the second language from outsiders. Two-thirds of the subjects who participated in the test were 
male. 
The RTT results for the Brave Hunter story in standard Sadri among the Dihari Korwa show an 
average score of 86 percent, with a low standard deviation of 8. These results suggest that many 
Dihari Korwa testees understood the story well but that a few had difficulties. The post-RTT 
responses show that almost all the subjects understood the story. The testees also report that this text 
is in the Sadri variety which is spoken in Jharkhand and Orissa side. There is a little difference 
between this variety and their own, the local Sadri, they added. 
The RTT result for the Brave Hunter story in standard Sadri among the Kodaku testees show an 
average score of 73 percent with a standard deviation of 13. This indicates that many testees could 
not understand the story well, but that a few are able to answer correctly. The post-RTT responses 
and observations support the fact that Kodaku people are not good at using standard Sadri. On the 
other hand, most of them reported the ability to handle the local Sadri very well. However, 
researchers did not test local Sadri comprehension among the Kodaku people to confirm this. 
4.3 
Conclusions 
Acknowledging the limitations of the RTT methodology for bilingualism testing, the following 
conclusions are drawn. 
The Pahadi Korwa subjects (who were mostly male) were able to understand the recorded story 
in local Sadri. However, because it is fairly easy to pass an RTT without a high level of proficiency in 
the L2, it is not possible to draw any conclusions as to how proficient in Sadri those subjects are who 
achieved high comprehension scores. 
The Dihari Korwa subjects speak a local variety of Sadri as their mother tongue. The subjects 
understood the standard Sadri story well. They identified the RTT Sadri variety as the one spoken in 
Jharkhand and Orissa side; they also submitted that this variety is only a little different from their 
own. 
The Kodaku subjects had a low level of understanding of the standard Sadri story. Most subjects 
reported, however, to understand local Sadri well, but no testing was done to confirm this.

Final conclusions 
5.1 
Language vitality 
Pahadi Korwa: In four of the five surveyed Pahadi Korwa settlements, people speak Pahadi Korwa on 
a daily basis. In the fifth village, however, the Korwa are shifting from Pahadi Korwa to Sadri. 
Overall, however, the Pahadi Korwa subjects from all five villages reported positive attitudes toward 
their mother tongue and seem to be interested in developing their language. 
Dihari Korwa: The Dihari Korwa subjects reported using Sadri in all domains and having 
positive attitudes toward the variety of Sadri they speak. They do not share any significant 
sociolinguistic affinity with the Pahadi Korwa. 
Kodaku: The use of Kodaku is higher in Chhattisgarh as compared with Jharkhand. The research 
findings suggest that language shift is in progress among the Kodaku people of Jharkhand; at the 
same time, however, they hold a positive attitude toward their mother tongue. The formation of 
Jharkhand as a separate state has put emphasis on developing minority languages, which, in turn, 
has revived the languages in the state. Hence there is a strong possibility of revitalization for Kodaku, 
too.
