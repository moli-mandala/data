Among Speaking Pahadi Korwa, the use of the mother tongue in community domains (such as 
the home, with people of the same caste, children while playing, in marriage songs) is high. More 
than three-quarters of the subjects (76–89%) reported that they use Korwa, their mother tongue, in 
these domains. On the other hand, the LWC Sadri is used more in other domains, such as the market, 
government offices, and in conversations with people from other castes. 
Around one-quarter of the Pahadi Korwa subjects fall into the Shifting category. Respondents 
from the Shifting Pahadi Korwa village reported using Sadri in all domains. Those who said they use 
Korwa are mostly subjects who are 70–80 years old. 
 The reported language use data indicate that those Korwa speakers who are shifting to Sadri 
have comparatively more access to the outside world than those Pahadi Korwa still using Korwa as 
their mother tongue. However, Sadri also has an influence on the Speaking Pahadi Korwa. Overall, 
the frequent use of Sadri and the fact that some Pahadi Korwa have already begun using Sadri almost 
exclusively gives some indications of a possible language shift among Speaking Pahadi Korwa, but 
there is a significant segment who are still using their mother tongue. Thus, language shift is not 
inevitable. 
2.1.2 
Among the Dihari Korwa 
A couple of questions in relation to language use were administered in Kotagohna, a Dihari Korwa 
village. Out of 19 subjects, all subjects reported that they widely use Sadri (or Surguja, an alternative 
name). In fact, the respondents reported Sadri as their mother tongue, and that they use it in every 
sphere of communication. A few respondents also stated that their children use Hindi in school. The 
researchers’ observations also concur with the subjects’ responses that the Dihari Korwa people do 
not have a separate mother tongue called Korwa.
