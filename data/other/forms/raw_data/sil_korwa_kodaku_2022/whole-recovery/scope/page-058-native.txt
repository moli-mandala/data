Some modifications to the lexical similarity grouping procedures summarised in Blair (1990) 
were also applied to the wordlists compared in this study. The need for this came about for several 
reasons. First, the wordlists were often not checked with a second mother tongue speaker of each 
speech variety. Second, the wordlists could not always be consistently elicited. In addition, the field 
workers’ phonetic transcription ability varied according to their skill, experience, and their own 
language background. 
B.3 
Modifications in the grouping procedure 
The following modifications were made while grouping the wordlist. As seen in table 13, comparison 
between two-segment words must have both segments in category 1. Since the rationale for the 
distribution is that at least half of the segments compared should be category 1, this principle is 
applied to two segment words so that a distribution of 1-1-0 was considered similar. 
• Word final [h] is not considered: 8, 18. 
• Intervocalic [h] is not considered in a specific case: 138. 
• Aspirated and unaspirated sounds are considered as category 1. 
• A nasalized vowel with nasal consonants is considered as category 1. (Specific cases) 
• Metathesis is applied in the following glosses: 16, 19, 28, 32, 46, 48, 52, 71, 105, 142, 144, 
151, 153–163, 167. 
• Root-based grouping is applied in the following glosses: 20, 21, 34, 42, 110, 111, 118, 128, 
131, 132, 136, 137, 143, 153, 164, 167, 175, 177, 179, 182, 183, 184, 193, 200. 
• Words that sounded similar are grouped together: 49, 51, 90, 108, 137, 177, 180, 183, 184, 
192, 194. 
• A glide in the Word is not considered in specific cases. 
• Double consonants are treated as single phones.
