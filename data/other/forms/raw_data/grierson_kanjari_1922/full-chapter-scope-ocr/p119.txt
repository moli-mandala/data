KUCHBANDHI.

The Kuchbandhis are a subdivision of the Kanjars. They make the hitch, or brush
used by weavers for cleaning thread, and also apply themselves to other of the usual
occupations of the Kanjars. A specimen of the so-called Kuchbandhi dialect has been
forwarded from the Bahraich District of the United Provinces, the principal language of
which is Awadhi. The first lines of it will, however, be sufficient to show that this
Kuchbandhi simply is a mixture of Awadhi and Rajasthini of the same kind as ordinary
Kanjari. Note the genitive suffix rd and the word gihar, man. Some remarks about
the argot of the Kuchbandhis will be found in Mr. Kirkpatrick’s paper quoted under
Authorities above on p. 97. It is of exactly the same kind as the secret language
of other Kanjars, and, like similar argots, it is called Parsi, or, according to Mr.
Kirkpatrick, Pasi.
