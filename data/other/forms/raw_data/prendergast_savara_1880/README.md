# Prendergast's complete Savara vocabulary (prepared 1880; printed 1881)

The primary source is “Short Vocabulary of the Savara Language” in *Journal of the Royal Asiatic Society*, new series 13 (1881), printed pp. 425–428. Robert Cust's dated introduction on p. 425 says M. H. Prendergast prepared the vocabulary in 1880 while Assistant Superintendent of Police at Vizagapatam, sent it to Rev. J. Cain, and Cain transmitted it to Cust. The preceding Koi paradigms are Cain's separate article and are excluded. No speaker or precise Savara elicitation site is given.

Original scan: https://archive.org/details/journalofroyalas1318roya (public-domain 1881 volume), SHA-256 `82965585bf86961eda337bcfdf48263f425092ce22a2589d00bebf6dcce603d1`. The scan and page renders are local review cache, not installed assets. The printed pages 426–428 are PDF physical pages 472–474 in that scan; p. 425 contains attribution rather than Savara entries.

The two inventory TSVs account for **all 266 printed prompt/response cells**: p. 426 left 59, p. 426 right 56, p. 427 left 59, p. 427 right 60, p. 428 left 23, p. 428 right 9. All are represented in the source-local audit and installed as 270 rows because four cells print two alternatives. The original p. 426-left 50-row pilot is retained with stable keys. The 1881 source includes short lexical prompts, pronouns, numerals, inflected forms and sentence examples; the latter are preserved as phrases rather than silently discarded. Four printed alternatives are separate child rows linked to their first alternative. Printed English parenthetical explanations within phrase forms are removed from the display form and retained verbatim in the audit.

The source maps to canonical Sora (`so`) with a source-qualified historical Savara dialect and blank coordinates. The preservation profile retains the printed romanization, macrons, and underdots without imposing a modern phonetic interpretation. The horse and rice readings remain visibly uncertain and carry `uncertain` tags; all cells are nevertheless accounted for and no source phrase is dropped. No native script, source phonemic analysis, etymological claim, or borrowing relation is provided. All 270 rows were screened against 2,171 older Sora source rows; twelve exact-form matches occur, including one divergent gloss (`mā` “to come” here versus “Go” in Grierson). No direct elicitation dependence is established by form identity.
