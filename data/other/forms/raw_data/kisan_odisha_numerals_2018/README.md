# Kisan (Odisha) numerals, two 2018 contributor tables

The primary page is Eugene Chan's [Kisan, Odisha numeral list](https://lingweb.eva.mpg.de/channumerals/Kisan.htm), represented here by `raw/Kisan_Odisha.htm` from the [numeralbank/channumerals v1.0.1 archive](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15649312). Its `raw/README.md` dates the download **12 February 2019**, and its `LICENSE` is **CC BY 4.0**. The exact archived HTML (50,764 bytes; SHA256 `a65613810ac0989604a473da70ca6b05fc1751947179008d33312abf0db969a6`) controls this import. It credits **Dr. Anup Kumar Kujur**, 12 August 2018, for the first table and **Dr. P. Perumalsamy**, 10 August 2018, for the second; the latter additionally cites his 2002 Kisan survey. The two tables are separate attestations within one archived page, not inferred independent dialects.

Both tables explicitly label the lect **Kisan, Odisha**. [Glottolog's `kisa1261`](https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/id/kisa1261) treats Kisan (Dravidian) as a dialect under Kurux. Jambu's canonical ID is `Kurux` (`kuru1301`); Kurukh is an alternate spelling. A source-level Kisan dialect under Kurux is appropriate. Jambu's unrelated Indo-Aryan `KisanIA` and its five Nepal survey dialects are not affected. The source names only Odisha broadly, with no exact site or coordinate. Compiled Kurux has 4,969 prior rows and several numeral attestations, but no registered Dravidian Kisan dialect; these are new source-specific Kisan attestations rather than a replacement for generic Kurux numerals.

The page has **82 explicit numbered units**: 42 in the Kujur table and 40 in the Perumalsamy table. The second table has 12 truly blank units: 21–29, 800, 1000, 2000. Each table repeats printed **4** in the row where a reader might expect 5; both repeated-4 answers are **held** and no 5 is fabricated. The remaining 68 numbered units yield **73 installed readings**, because five Kujur cells print two comma-separated answers (10, 19, 20, 60, 1000). Those readings have distinct stable entry keys, without a directional variant relation. All 82 units are in `audit.jsonl`; the 20-unit seeded audit reports zero material extraction errors, and the table starts, ends, blank runs, repeated-4 rows, and multi-answer cells were checked separately.

The source explicitly says 1–4 are Dravidian-origin and subsequent numerals are Indo-Aryan loans. Installed numerals above 4 carry the source-level `loanword` tag. No donor language, etymon, or graph edge is invented. The two source 4 readings are installed with `uncertain` because of fractured internal spacing; five Kujur compounds with a length marker stranded after a space are also marked `uncertain`. Their raw spelling is preserved, and the audit gives the typed transcription concern. The profile maps clear IPA length to macrons, retroflexes to house letters, Kujur's affricate notation to `c`, and Perumalsamy's printed `c` to house `ʦ` in line with the page's notation key. The original forms and Perumalsamy's bracketed IPA are retained separately. No OCR was used. Chinese translations, grammar prose, arithmetic explanations, and the cited 2002 work itself are excluded from the lexical extraction.

`import_source.py` checks the archived HTML hash and deterministically regenerates the audit and dated installed CSV. The installed CSV/YAML, sound profile, dialect, and BibTeX record are present. Focused importer/dialect tests pass (12), `source_meta.py` validates the registry, the whole-source-inventory profile audit has no finding for this profile, and a scoped `make_cldf.parse_file` smoke check converts all 73 rows without error.
