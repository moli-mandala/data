The source is the complete article, printed pp.149–172, in *Journal of the Asiatic Society of Bengal*69(2), authored by Ferdinand Hahn and communicated by G.A.Grierson. The printed volume/article date is 1900; some digitization catalogs use 1901. The 218-page Wikimedia/Internet Archive scan is public domain; its SHA 256 is in `manifest.json`. Printed page+12 gives the one-based PDF page.

`reviewed_inventory.tsv` preserves the original 31-cell p 170 comparison inventory and all 29 previously installed stable keys. `full_inventory.tsv` covers the remainder of the article:439 selected lexical/paradigm cells,4 held items,2 same-source reuse records and 24 page-context scope audits. Together 474 target cells (468 selected,6 held) produce 621 form rows. The retained `article-ocr.txt` includes every article page; all transcription and exclusions can be checked against an exact page/section/item citation. `import_source.py --install` regenerates the headerless 15-column source CSV and 500-record `audit.jsonl`.

Coverage includes the six introductory totem glosses, rice-beer, gender/number forms, noun/pronoun/verb paradigms,20 possessive kin terms, participles/infinitives, adverb/numeral/postposition/conjunction lists and the complete closing comparative vocabulary. Possessive kin forms are retained as printed rather than converted into invented bare kinship lemmas. Explicit paradigm alternatives become separate keyed rows; later alternatives point to the first via `Variant_Of_Key`. Repeated pronouns/stems are expanded only where the source explicitly abbreviates a coordinated paradigm; the page OCR preserves the abbreviation.

Connected syntactic illustrations, ethnographic prose, contents, the closing song, bound morphological signs and Mundari/Kurukh controls are excluded explicitly. Sentence examples are not reverse-engineered into unattested lexical roots. Four extension holds cover a divine-name transcription, an unglossed interrogative declension string, an unglossed future-perfect auxiliary and a completive marker. The original table retains two historical holds: a plural ending and a paired deictic comparison. The source's proposed Kurukh comparisons are preserved as qualified etymological prose; no borrowing or cognacy is inferred. Kurukh *iŋyō* is not an Asur alternative for *eŋā*.

The source-qualified registered Asuri dialect retains blank coordinates: the article names broad communities but no unique speaker/site for these forms. The source preservation profile retains macrons, retroflex letters, hiatus apostrophes and the distinction between ruled *ṉ* and *ŋ* described on p 153. In particular it does not silently turn the two historical nasal signs into one modern phonological analysis. `Original` preserves the source spelling through the normal CSV pipeline; no duplicated IPA field is invented.
