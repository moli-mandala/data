## Transcription and scholarly qualifications

The Nordic XML and separate table layer supplied keyed initial prose/paradigm text, not OCR. Every staged literal was checked against the DSAL grayscale original. All seven specimen pages have an owner second reading; the complete independent rereading of all 706 atoms has been reconciled. The 241 table cells have a complete independent original-image review. Immutable initial readings and each keyed correction report are preserved.

Grierson explicitly says long and short vowels interchange, and compares o/u variants (p. 333). Printed differences remain separate: they are not harmonized across repeated forms, paradigms or comparison languages. The profile preserves macrons, nasal and underdot marks, diaeresis and underbarred consonants. It applies Jambu's lowercase, w→v and ṅ→ŋ conventions and removes editorial punctuation in normalized Form. Original retains literal notation. No reconstructed phonetics or automatic alternate splitting is introduced.

The p. 334 pronunciation discussion limits the palatal-affricate statement to at least Sholapur and conditions it on the following vowel/glide. The same page compares h/p and the loss of final ḷ with other languages. These are attributed source-level descriptions, not unconditional conversion rules. Underbarred printed consonants remain literal in Original and Form rather than being automatically expanded using those prose claims.

The genitive is said sometimes to agree in gender with its qualified noun. The source's statements about neuter forms used for masculine in Ellichpur and neuter singular apparently used as feminine are preserved as qualified analyses, not grounds for regularizing the attested forms. In particular, `nallād urtī` retains the literal gloss “a good woman” and the qualified neuter-as-feminine claim without a confident feminine morphology tag. The exclusive analysis of `nāṅg` remains tentative; explicitly inclusive Sholapur `nāmb` and `nāmburk` are distinguished. The source's interrogative and genitive labels on yāu/midā/ēdu/yattan are explicit metadata.

Tamil, Korava, Gondi, Telugu, Kannada and Marathi comparisons remain attributed. Grierson's historical classification of Kaikadi under Tamil is not substituted for the established Kaikadi/kaik1244 language identity. Specifically cited Marathi forms used in Kaikadi are distinguished from comparison-only Tamil forms. P. 335 claims involving Tamil past/future correspondences, possible Marathi influence, and the apparent analysis of Ellichpur forms remain claims rather than inferred derivations.

District identity follows explicit source attribution only: Sholapur, Akola, Buldana, Ahmednagar, Kolaba, Aundh, Ramdrug, Berar and Ellichpur. Buldana's specimen retains the narrower Melkapur Taluka note. General chapter paradigms receive no invented district. Historical labels are preserved without guessed coordinates.
