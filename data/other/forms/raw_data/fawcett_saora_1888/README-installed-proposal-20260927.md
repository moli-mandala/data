## Transcription and interpretation

Fawcett states on p. 227 that an unnamed Uriya speaker who knew Saora first wrote the tables; he then reviewed pronunciation with an unnamed Saora speaker. He warns that exact pronunciation cannot be conveyed and calls his notation the Missionary alphabet. No table-specific speaker locality is provided. The proposal uses canonical Sora `so` and does not invent a dialect or precise coordinates. Its profile preserves the historical vowel marks and syllable commas, accepts equivalent NFC/NFD input, lowercases display forms, and applies house `w → v`; it does not claim that the historical notation is IPA. Printed Original forms remain intact. Citation-specific `forms.split_alternates: false` prevents generic parsing from splitting syllable commas.

Complete possessive kinship expressions retain printed `Mian` on p. 226 and `Main` on p. 227. Source `m. s.`/`f. s.` labels and stress footnotes remain in Notes; no grammatical gender is inferred from speaker labels. Three nested English ditto scopes remain explicitly unresolved in the gloss, and p. 226 row 32 retains the source's trailing possessive “son’s.” Its explicit `or` yields two complete alternatives. Independent comparison confirmed corrections `thon,am,ba`, `balarn vulengo`, and `navonu` in three p. 227 rows.

Personal and deity names are retained as proper names with their qualifications. Documentary subgroup labels such as “Arisi Saoras” remain complete in the audit because their linguistic attribution is unstated; “Saoras” is not stripped to invent a lemma. Telugu/Uriya comparisons are not promoted to Sora. Qualified borrowing claims are retained as source claims with uncertainty, without invented donor edges. Literal contrasts including `Thīmji`, `Mïgŭl`, `Sherràka`, `Limmă`, and `Lŏbō` are preserved.
