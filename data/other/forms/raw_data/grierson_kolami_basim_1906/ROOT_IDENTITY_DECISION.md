# Historical Basim routing decision

Root independently inspected the original LSI IV p. 561 and specimen 76 header
on p. 568, then checked the primary DEDR front matter at
https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/burrow/frontmatter/frontmatter.html
on 27 September 2026. Section 27 explicitly equates Naikṛi with LSI's Bhili of
Basim; section 28 distinguishes Naiki of Chanda. The original specimen labels
Basim's speech as Dravidian. This direct bibliographic identification supports
routing the historical Basim material beneath existing canonical `Naikri`.

Current Jambu registry distinguishes `Naikri/sout1549`, `Naiki/naik1250` and
`Kolami/nort2699`. Reuse those distinctions; do not route Basim to Indo-Aryan
Bhili or Naiki of Chanda, or silently replace the registry with DEDR's umbrella
classification of Naikṛi as a Kolami dialect. DEDR's classification is retained
as source evidence and is not an instruction to collapse existing base languages.

Propose a registered, language-qualified historical Basim survey lect under
Naikri. Its display label should retain the source's “so-called Bhili” wording
and district attribution. Exact elicitation coordinates and lect-specific
Glottocode remain blank unless independently established. Existing modern base
coordinates must not be presented as historical fieldwork coordinates.

Wun Kolami remains a separate source-qualified historical lect proposal beneath
existing Kolami. Do not reuse the Lexibank LSI lect or Kinwat/Pandharkavra/Waghapur
lects merely because they share the base language. Grierson's whole chapter must
establish the exact attribution of each form or collective comparison.

The five Basim identity holds in the installed Kui audit remain immutable
historical evidence. The new complete source must identify the same physical
occurrences, record the later identity decision and preserve reuse/overlap
relationships without silently modifying or double-counting existing evidence.
No canonical language, dialect, bibliography or source rows are changed by this
decision. Metadata implementation and candidate acceptance remain separate gates.
