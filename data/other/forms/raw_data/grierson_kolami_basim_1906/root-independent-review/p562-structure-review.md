# Independent p562 structure review

Root compared the complete original p562 top/bottom controls with the 80
independent child interpretations in `independent-grammar-review/literal-witnesses.tsv`.
This is a structural review, not acceptance of every fine glyph or the final importer.

The original supports these interpretation boundaries:

- The grammar is supplemented from two additional parable versions and a standard
  list. It is not attributable in its entirety to the Wun specimen lect. Only one
  version had a translation; Grierson supplied the printed interlinear translation.
- Tamil and Kanarese share one printed `gaḷ` comparator. Kanarese and Telugu share
  one printed `eidu` comparator. Each is one physical printing with two explicit
  language attributions, not two physical source tokens.
- Masculine numeral labels immediately precede the masculine alternatives `ōkōn`
  and `iddar`; they do not retroactively classify the preceding alternatives.
- The personal pronoun table has four gloss groups in its left block, three in
  its middle block, and five in its right block. `he` and `it` are separate gloss
  groups on one physical line; `they` and its neuter alternative share one group.
- Parentheses in the accusative pronoun and present copula are literal printed
  notation. Automatically manufacturing both optional spellings would add
  unattested printings.
- The statement about no feminine noun examples supplies no feminine forms. The
  statements about plural/singular substitution and absent real plural present
  copula examples qualify the evidence; they do not authorize additional paradigms.
- The past copula has six printed person/number cells. Empty lexical glosses may
  remain intentional where the source provides only grammatical labels.
- Case suffixes and the qualified plural `ur, r` are actual printed markers. Their
  descriptions belong in structured grammatical tags or source analysis, not a
  fabricated lexical definition or invented leading hyphen.
