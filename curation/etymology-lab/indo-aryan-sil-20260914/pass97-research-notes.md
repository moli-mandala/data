- Stone hil/sil uses Assamese xil and Bengali sil under CDIAL 12459 sense 1. The homepage's Maldivian hila match belongs to the *śillā addendum and is not the justification for the eastern links.
- Bare has/hãs ‘laugh’ is linked to 14021. Forms beginning jun-/nei-/nai-/mata- and forms with additional verbal material require fuller grammatical analysis. They are not marked adjudicated by this pass.
- Cut links cover bare kaṭ and Bengali kaʈa. Dental-looking kata variants and longer inflected/negative responses remain outside this batch; similarity is not sufficient to establish whether their notation preserves or obscures the expected retroflex.
- The vegetable noun sag/sāg has the voiced stop explicitly present in Prakrit sāga. The one nasalised sãg is qualified in its evidence.
- Ginger aduva/əduwa uses the specific Nepali aduwā comparison. Retroflex-marked ginger forms and aruva remain outside this exact set.
- Do/make kor/kora/kore/kura/kɔɾa belongs to 2814. Other survey forms, including koye and bana, are not automatically folded into that root.
