# Compound verb construction evidence

Read 21 September 2026. Primary dictionary text: Platts (1884), reproduced under the explicitly labelled **PLATTS DICTIONARY** section at Rekhta. These are construction comparanda; they do not establish a Hindi borrowing into each surveyed language.

- [marnā](https://www.rekhta.org/urdudictionary?keyword=marnaa): separately lists mar-jānā as a death verb and notes that the compound is commonly used for relevant senses of the simple verb.
- [mārnā](https://www.rekhta.org/urdudictionary?keyword=maarnaa): explicitly lists mār-denā as an intensive construction. This supports analysing the survey's perfect give auxiliary separately from kill.
- [khānā](https://www.rekhta.org/urdudictionary?keyword=khaanaa): separately lists both khā-jānā and khā-lenā as intensive eating constructions.

The historical identities and perfect-stem distinctions come from the complete CDIAL articles and addenda saved in `primary-018.json`. Candidate discovery used the actual homepage search, recorded in `search-023.json`. The rules and record selection are in `pass008-rules.json` and `pass008-decisions.json`.

The component links identify the comparative families of the members of these modern constructions. They do not assert that the constructions themselves descend intact from Sanskrit compounds. Exact local grammatical and contact histories remain open. No lexical records or spelling profiles are changed.
