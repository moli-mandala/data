# Darai: buy, youngest, and lazy

Three source-backed reflex records across three etyma.

| # | Darai form | Proposed etymology | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **kinlarə** ‘buy’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 61]) | **krīṇā́ti**, CDIAL **3594** | Dhakal p. 61 prints /kin.la.rə/ “buy.PROS”; p. 56 identifies -larʰə as the prospective marker. The root kin- matches Turner3594, which gives Nepali kinnu “to buy,” Maithili kinab and Bhojpuri kinal. The prospective suffix is Darai morphology, not part of the etymon. |
| 2 | **kanʦʰə** ‘the youngest one’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 61]) | **kanyasa-**, CDIAL **2735** | Dhakal p. 61 prints /kan.cʰə/ “the youngest one.” Turner2735 gives Nepali kāncho “younger, the youngest brother,” and its addenda Jaunsari kaṇchā “younger, youngest.” This is the affricate branch of kanyasa-, not the separate kaniṣṭhá article 2718. |
| 3 | **əlʦʰi** ‘lazy’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 62]) | **ā́lasya**, CDIAL **1371** | Dhakal p. 62 prints /əl.cʰi/ “lazy.” Turner1371 lists adjective descendants of ālasya, including Nepali alchi “lazy,” alongside Punjabi ālsī and Oriya āḷasi. The Darai affricate adjective matches the Nepali form closely. |
