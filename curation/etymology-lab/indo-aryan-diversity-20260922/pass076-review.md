# Darai: descriptors and cheek

Five distinct source-backed reflex records, preserving the source vowel and consonant contrasts.

| # | Darai form | Proposed etymology | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **bəl** ‘strength’ | **bala**, CDIAL **9161** | Dhakal p. 44 explicitly contrasts central-vowel /bəl/ strength with /bal/ light. Turner9161 gives Pali/Prakrit bala strength and Nepali, Maithili and Bihari bal; choose only the strength homonym and preserve the source vowel distinction. |
| 2 | **ʦʰõṭo** ‘short’ | ***chōṭṭa**, CDIAL **5071** | Dhakal p. 46 prints /cʰõṭo/ short and describes its nasal vowel. Turner5071 gives Kumaoni choṭo, Kashmiri ċhoṭu short/small and Nepali choṭo small under *chōṭṭa. The source nasalization remains as printed; the data do not determine a contact-free path. |
| 3 | **naŋṭəi** ‘naked’ | **nagna**, CDIAL **6926** | Dhakal p. 61 prints /naŋ.ṭəi/ naked. Turner6926 gives Maithili nā̃gaṭ, Hindi nãgṭā and Bengali neṅṭā, and explicitly notes deformations in this word family. The Darai -əi ending is preserved without claiming a specific historical suffix. |
| 4 | **dubro** ‘thin’ | **durbala**, CDIAL **6438** | Dhakal p. 68 prints /dub.ro/ thin. Turner6438 gives Hindi dūbrā and Awadhi dūbar beside Nepali dublo and Bhojpuri dū̆bar under durbala. The r-bearing Darai outcome has regional comparanda, so it is not inferred from Nepali l by itself. |
| 5 | **galu** ‘cheek’ | **galla**, CDIAL **4089** | Dhakal p. 67 prints /ga.lu/ cheek. Turner4089 gives Prakrit galla, Old Awadhi gālu, Oriya gāla and Bihari/Maithili gāl. The exact vowel ending is visible in the Old Awadhi comparison; this is the cheek family, not gala throat. |
