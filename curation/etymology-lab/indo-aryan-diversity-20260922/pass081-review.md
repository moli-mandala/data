# Darai: five verbal and agricultural families

Six source-backed reflex records across five etyma.

| # | Darai form | Proposed etymology | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **ʦirike** ‘to split’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 54]) | ***cīrayati**, CDIAL **4844** | Dhakal p. 54 prints /cirike/ “to split,” contrasting /cʰirike/ “to enter.” Turner4844 lists Nepali cirnu, Bengali cirā, Oriya ciribā and Maithili cīrab “to split”; Darai -ike is the source-described ordinary infinitive. |
| 2 | **ʣuʣʰike** ‘to fight’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 54]) | **yúdhyatē**, CDIAL **10502** | Dhakal p. 54 prints /dzudzʰike/ “to fight.” Turner10502 gives Pali yujjhati, Bengali jujhā, Oriya jujhibā and Bhojpuri jūjhal. The Darai infixal affricate and aspirate match the regional jujh- stem; -ike is the ordinary infinitive. |
| 3 | **ʣuʣʰlə** ‘fought’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 68]) | **yúdhyatē**, CDIAL **10502** | Dhakal p. 68 prints /dzudzʰlə/ “fought”; p. 75 identifies -lə as the past suffix. It is an inflection of the same jujh- stem that Turner10502 compares with eastern jujh-/jūjh- forms, so it is linked directly as a reflex, not a new derivation. |
| 4 | **rokike** ‘to stop’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 56]) | ***rōkk-**, CDIAL **10827** | Dhakal p. 56 contrasts /rokike/ “to stop” with /lokike/ “to hide.” Turner10827 lists Nepali roknu, Bengali rokā, Oriya rokibā and Maithili rokab; Darai -ike is its ordinary infinitive suffix. Turner leaves the remote relation to rudh uncertain, which this link does not assert. |
| 5 | **pʰar** ‘metal plough tip’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 51]) | **phā́la**, CDIAL **9072** | Dhakal p. 51 prints /pʰar/ “metal plough tip.” Turner9072 explicitly gives Bihari, Maithili and Bhojpuri phār “small ploughshare,” alongside Oriya phāra. This agricultural sense selects 9072 rather than the generic blade family 9052. |
| 6 | **kaṭi** ‘cut’ (dhakal2011darai[p. 52]) | **kártati**, CDIAL **2854** | Dhakal p. 52 prints /kaṭi/ “cut.ABS”; the installed gloss abbreviates this to “cut.” Turner2854 lists Maithili kāṭab, Bihari kāṭab and Oriya kāṭibā “to cut”; the Darai -i is the source response’s absolutive inflection of that verbal stem, not a separate etymon. |
