# Zargari: in front and from the front

Three source-checked reflex records in one explicit Turner Romani family. Standing autonomous authorization. Original PDF printed pp. 128, 132, 143–144, glyph audit and imported keys, homepage searches and full CDIAL article/addenda were checked. No lexical import or rebuild.

| Zargari forms and meanings | Proposed etymology | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **ānglāl / ānglo / ānglo** ‘from the front; front / in front; in front of’ | ***agrala- (ágra extended with -la-)**, CDIAL **68-2x** | Turner 68, specifically its -la- extension represented by the compiled node 68-2x, explicitly cites European Romani angle 'in front' and anglal 'from in front'. The source prints Zargari ānglāl [ānglār] 'from the front' while documenting liquid dissimilation (§2.3.9, p. 128), then āngl-āl 'from the front' (§3.1.15, p. 132). It lists ānglo 'front; in front' as an adverb (§3.20.1.3, p. 143) and 'in front of' as an adposition (§3.20.2.1, p. 144). These are direct matches to the two Turner Romani comparanda, with the source-defined adverbial and ablative forms retained. |

Three held records:
- **nāy** ‘finger’: The source prints 'finger' (p. 129), while Turner 6914 directly gives European Romani nai 'fingernail'. A later Romani survey has nay 'nail; finger', but the Zargari semantic extension is not established by Turner itself.
- **somlāl** ‘gold’: The source prints 'gold' (p. 128) under liquid dissimilation; Turner 13519 has related Romani sovnakay/somnakay, but the full -lāl versus -nakay correspondence is not explained by the source.
- **bārbāyla** ‘wind’: The source prints this 'wind' form (p. 131), while Turner 11491 marks the Romani balval/bārval comparison with question marks and cannot securely distinguish vātá from vāyú. Hold the precise origin.
