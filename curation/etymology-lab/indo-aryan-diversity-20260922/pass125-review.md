| # | Zargari form and meaning | Proposal | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **śel** ‘one hundred’ | **reflex**, Turner[12278 and addenda] | Turner 12278 śatá '100' explicitly cites European Romani šel. The source prints Zargari śel 'one hundred' in its numeral table (p. 137 §3.5.1) and explains the -i- connector before following numerals (p. 138 §3.5.1.1). This is the simple hundred stem. |
| 2 | **duy-śel** ‘two hundred’ | **component**, Turner[6648];Turner[12278] | The source prints duy-śel 'two hundred' (p. 138 §3.5.1) and explicitly states that numerals are compounded (§3.5.1.1). The component edge records this composition, not a separate whole-word Sanskrit ancestor. |
| 3 | **śel-i-pānj** ‘one hundred and five’ | **component**, Turner[12278];Turner[7655] | The source explicitly prints śel-i-pānj 'one hundred and five' (p. 138 §3.5.1.1) as its example of -i- before the following numeral. Its two source lexical components are śel 'hundred' (newly linked to CDIAL 12278) and pānj 'five' (already linked to CDIAL 7655); -i- remains the source connector. This records components, not a single ancient compound etymon. |

Two held records:
- **śel-sila** ‘one hundred thousand’: The printed hundred–thousand composition is clear (p. 138), but the source's sila 'thousand' is not established as Indo-Aryan and may be Greek. Hold a full component analysis pending the immediate origin of sila.
- **śel-i-peyindā-sārāndā-śov** ‘one hundred and ninety-six’: The printed '196' form contains the known śel and śov elements but also mixed tens with unresolved contact histories. Do not assign a whole-word historical origin based on the two secure pieces alone.
