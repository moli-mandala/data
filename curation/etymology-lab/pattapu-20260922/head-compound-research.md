# Pattapu θalakāya ‘head’: full-form evidence

The existing DEDR discovery is retained in body-results.json. Full articles3103 (tala/talai head) and1459 (kāy/kāya fruit/nut) were read earlier. The remaining question concerns the relation type and full formation, not resemblance to either component.

External checks:

- [Percival, Telugu-English Dictionary (1862), printed p.225, digitized page237](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Telugu-English_Dictionary.djvu/237) transcribes tala and talakaya together as head. The [volume index](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Index:Telugu-English_Dictionary.djvu) identifies Percival,1862, Public Instruction Press, Madras. This page is explicitly unproofread; the linked scan request failed with a cache miss, so the image has not been verified. Do not misattribute this dictionary to Brown.
- A [third-party Brown transcription, p.515](https://www.telugufont.com/telugu-to-english-alpha.php?key=%E0%B0%A4%E0%B0%B2%E0%B0%95%E0%B0%BE%E0%B0%AF.) says talakāya means head and describes kāya in this use as a colloquial affix. This was search-result evidence, not independently checked against Brown's scan. It raises the distinction between lexical compound components and an affixal/derivational extension; it does not establish the Pattapu analysis by itself.
- The [official DSAL Brown landing page](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/dictionaries/brown/) was accessible and confirms the1903 edition. The attempted page515 endpoint was inaccessible to the web tool; a local curl attempt failed DNS resolution. No entry text was obtained there.

Current decision: hold the Pattapu record while confirming the primary entry and relation type. The whole form has external attestation support, but do not claim a proven Telugu borrowing route, equate semantic bleaching with a new inherited component automatically, or introduce a donor node. Existing head and fruit records are unchanged.
