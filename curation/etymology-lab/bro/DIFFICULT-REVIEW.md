# Brokskat: unresolved source checks

These are research holds, not proposed or accepted assignment rows. They remain in the unresolved count. The [primary grammar](https://pahar.in/pahar/Books%20and%20Articles/Indian%20Subcontinent/1998%20Tribal%20Languages%20of%20Ladakh%20part%20A--concise%20Grammar%20and%20Dictionary%20of%20Brok-skad%20by%20Sharma%20s.pdf) was read in searchable text; its unreliable OCR prevents treating uncertain phonetic symbols as verified facsimile readings. CDIAL comparisons were checked in full local dictionary prose.

| Brokskat | Candidates | Why unresolved |
|---|---|---|
| **rus** ‘to cry’ | CDIAL [10840](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=627), subsections 1 rōdati and 2 rudati | The compiled rus favors ru-, but both ro- and ru- are reported in the grammar. The comparative article documents secondary vowel changes even among subsection 1 forms; a single normalized vowel does not settle the historical subsection. |
| **dus** ‘to wash (tr.)’ | CDIAL [6803](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=389) dhāvati, [6833](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=391) *dhuvati, [6886](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=395) *dhauvati | Compiled dus has u, whereas the grammar lists do-. Turner distinguishes these remodeled presents and the old dhāvati stem. The broad root connection is plausible but insufficient to select one exact parent. |
| **byās** ‘to go’ | No parent selected | The grammar separates this stem from its explicit Old Indo-Aryan root list. A general comparison to Shina bo- is a lead, not enough evidence to resolve its derivation or any contact history. |
| **skis** ‘to see’ | CDIAL [5716](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=325) tarkayati considered, not assigned | The cluster in skis is not an automatic match to Gilgit c̣ak-. The grammar likewise leaves the stem outside its explicit inherited list. Need a historical account of the cluster and independent Brokskat evidence. |
| **'āe** ‘mother’ | CDIAL [1347](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=61) ārya versus [1351](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=61) āryikā and nursery alternatives | The grammar proposes āryā; Turner places Shina āže under āryikā and refers onward to *āī. Brokskat āe lacks the consonant of āže, so assigning the ordinary Shina parent without checking these alternatives would conceal the issue. |
| **apś** ‘Horse’ | CDIAL [920](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=40) aśva; possible Iranian intermediate | Turner explicitly records Brokskat apš under aśva. The grammar introduces an Iranian aspa stage, so inherited and borrowed histories remain competing analyses; no immediate Iranian donor node has yet been verified. |
| **'lōdo** ‘red’ | CDIAL [11165](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=650) lohita, [11168](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=650) *lohila, [10872](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=628) raudra | The grammar offers lohita and raudra comparisons. Turner prefers *lohila for Shina lōlyu but Brokskat lōdo has d, not l. A sound-history explanation is required before selecting a parent; raudra’s dictionary evidence concerns sunshine rather than this Brokskat color term. |
| **'āti, āṭī** ‘bone’ | CDIAL [958](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=42) aṣṭhi versus [982](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=43) asthi | Turner explicitly regards most of the listed Dardic bone terms, including Shina ā̃ṭi/aṭi, as Indo-Aryan loans. Brokskat āṭī resembles that family, but assigning a direct reflex of either Sanskrit head would conceal the donor-route problem. The plain-t Schmidt spelling also needs reconciliation. |
| **'bude, budeś** ‘twelve; 12’ | CDIAL [6658](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=380) dvādaśa versus subsection 2 duvādaśa | Brokskat budeś/bude twelve begins with b but retains an internal d absent from the familiar Shina bāi branch. An analogical renewal of daśa is possible, yet its exact history and subsection cannot be recovered solely from the numeral value. |
| **trobes, trøbeś** ‘thirteen; 13’ | CDIAL [6001](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=342) trayōdaśa versus subsection 2 *trayēdaśa | Brokskat trobes/trøbeś thirteen has internal b. Neither the long-o nor long-e historical branch directly explains this consonant; evidence for a shared Brokskat remodeling of teen numerals is needed before choosing one. |
| **'sobeś, ṣøbeś** ‘sixteen; 16’ | CDIAL [12812](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=743) ṣoḍaśa versus subsections 2 *ṣodaśa and 3 *ṣeḍaśa | Brokskat sobeś/ṣøbeś sixteen has internal b where Turner distinguishes retroflex, dental, and front-vowel historical formations. The neighboring ṣōweś type is a lead, not proof that Brokskat b reflects its w. |
| **'qudes, qudeś** ‘eleven; 11’ | CDIAL [2485](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=120) ekādaśa | Brokskat qudes/qudeś eleven plausibly belongs to the ekādaśa family, but initial q, vowel reduction, and preserved d need to be checked against the same teen-number remodeling before a specific historical path is proposed. |
| **'pādes, pændeś** ‘fifteen; 15’ | CDIAL [7662](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=432) pañcadaśa | The Batalik pændeś fifteen better preserves the nasal than Schmidt pādes. Compare Kashmiri pandāh and the broader pañcadaśa family, but the contrast among Brokskat teen formations makes the exact inherited versus rebuilt stem unresolved. |
| **'kunjā, kunʣ̣æ** ‘nineteen; 19’ | Subtractive nineteen formation; no parent selected | Brokskat kunjā/kunʣ̣æ nineteen should not be equated with ekonavimśati merely because of its value. Its internal structure remains to be established, and compounds containing it are likewise held unresolved. |
| **tīto** ‘bitter’ | Kashmiri loan; compare CDIAL [5938](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=339), versus withdrawn [5806](https://dsal.uchicago.edu/cgi-bin/app/soas_query.py?page=330) | Withdrawn proposal 66 had linked tīto directly to tikta (5806), but Schmidt–Kaul p. 247 explicitly classifies this Brokskat word as a Kashmiri loan. The available Kashmiri ṭyoṭhᵘ bitter belongs to CDIAL 5938 tṛṣṭa and differs in vowel and consonants; the article’s Kashmiri table has no main-dialect bitter response, and its Kishtawari record is merged with hot. An exact donor correspondence must be checked before replacing the withdrawn direct-reflex assignment. [Primary article](https://www.wisdomlib.org/uploads/journals/acta-orie/vol-69-2008/7372-6759-23258.pdf). |
