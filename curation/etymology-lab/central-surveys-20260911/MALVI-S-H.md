# Malvi s : h correspondence: internal source comparison

This is a comparison of the already ingested primary survey, not a new lexical import. The diplomatic source snapshot supplies four independent lexical families in the same localities. It supports treating the h-initial forms as local counterparts of s-initial forms; it does not imply that every Sanskrit sibilant becomes h in every Malvi dialect.

Source: Varghese, John and Samuel (2009), *The Malvi-speaking people of Madhya Pradesh and Rajasthan: A sociolinguistic profile*, Appendix B: printed p. 124, item 133 ‘dry’; p. 136, item 157 ‘seven’; p. 140, item 164 ‘one hundred’; p. 167, item 200 ‘listen!, he heard’. [Archived primary PDF](https://web.archive.org/web/20150930085157id_/http://www-01.sil.org/silesr/2009/silesr2009-011.pdf). The evidence below comes from the existing checked diplomatic snapshot, not from the unavailable live PDF reader.

The full response rows, including paired imperative/past utterances and the duplicated masculine/feminine dry entries, are preserved in [malvi-h-correspondence-records.json](malvi-h-correspondence-records.json). Repeated cells are not counted as independent lexical families. This comparison is independent of the survey’s lexical-similarity category numbers, which are not etymological judgments.

The checked CDIAL families are śuṣka (12548), sapta (13139), śata (12278) and śṛṇoti (12598). Together with the regional s- comparanda in those entries, the survey matrix supports a local historical s/ś > h interpretation. That historical direction is an inference from comparative evidence; the survey data directly establish the synchronic locality correspondence.

Merged nodes such as hāt ‘arm; palm; seven’ remain held: identifying the seven token does not authorize assigning a single etymology to the whole merged node. The h-pattern does not resolve such record-identity conflicts.
